Instrument 04

AI Visibility Scanner

Can an answer engine actually read your site?

Runs in about ten seconds · Scans one URL · Free, no forms, no email

Scans one public URL plus its /robots.txt, /sitemap.xml and /llms.txt. Nothing is stored and no report is published. Ten scans per hour.

Why this exists

The Visibility Self-Diagnostic asks you twenty-five questions about your own site. It is useful, and it has one structural weakness: you are the one answering. A self-report is only ever as honest as the person filling it in, and the failures that matter most are the ones nobody knows they have.

This instrument does not ask. It fetches the URL, parses what came back, and grades it.

Every deduction below is a mechanical fact about a document — the structured data parsed or it did not, the crawler is permitted or it is blocked, the text was in the HTML or it was not. Nothing here is a projection, and no number comes from a vendor. You can reproduce the entire report with curl.

How the grade works

The page starts at 100 and loses points for each failure, weighted by what the failure actually costs. A blocked GPTBot is not a ranking penalty — it is total absence from that engine, so it costs more than a missing meta description. Grades run A+ through F on the same bands the Mozilla Observatory uses, for the same reason: a letter is legible to someone who will never read the detail.

What a bad grade means

A low score is not a verdict on the business. It is the gap between what the business is and what a machine can determine about it — and that gap is a build problem, not a marketing one. Most of the deductions here are fixed once and stay fixed.
