Instruments Beat Pitches

A pitch asks someone to accept your framing. An instrument lets them measure themselves and arrive at it alone.

Published 25 July 2026 · 4 min read · Anchored to the instruments on this site

There is a moment in most sales conversations where you explain why the thing you noticed matters. The diagnosis is correct, and you are asking someone to take your word for it. They rarely do — not because they doubt you specifically, but because you are the party who benefits from the diagnosis being true. That is not cynicism on their part. It is hygiene.

A pitch and an instrument make the same claim by structurally different routes. A pitch asserts a problem, so its credibility rests entirely on the asserter, who is also the vendor; the prospect has to resolve a conflict of interest before they can evaluate the content at all. An instrument hands over the criteria and lets them apply it to their own situation. The conclusion comes from their inputs, so its authority does not depend on trusting you. What someone reaches by measuring themselves is not a claim they have to accept — it is a result they already own.

This only works if the instrument is real, and there are two ways to fake it. Rig the scale so everyone lands in a band that needs your help, and the first person who runs it against a site they know is well built will notice. Gate it behind an email form and it stops being an instrument: they paid for the ruler before seeing a number. An instrument has to be able to return a good score and tell someone they are fine. If it cannot do both, it is a pitch with arithmetic.
