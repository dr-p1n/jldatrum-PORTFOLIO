The Spreadsheet Is the CMS

You were told you need a real system. Usually you need a pipe, not a replacement.

Published 25 July 2026 · 5 min read · Anchored to Panama Treasures

A business keeps its inventory in a spreadsheet. Somebody sells them software to fix that. Eighteen months later the software is half-populated, the spreadsheet is still the file people actually update, and the operation maintains two contradicting sources of truth instead of one.

The diagnosis was wrong. The spreadsheet is kept by the person who handles the stock, structured into fields and records by years of daily friction, and current because updating it is the path of least resistance — the three hardest habits to instil in a team, already in place. It is the content management system. What it lacks is a publishing step: the data terminates on somebody’s machine instead of reaching anywhere a buyer or a machine can read it.

That is a far smaller failure than the one usually sold against, and a far cheaper one to fix. Replacement attacks the wrong layer — it asks the one person whose process works to adopt a slower interface for the same result. If a proposed system does not make the maintainer’s day easier it will lose to the spreadsheet, and it should. Build the pipe, not the replacement.
